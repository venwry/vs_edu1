What changes the result most

  • Custom silicon yield: a 50% die-yield reduction doubles wafer demand. The 5% reserve does not absorb it.
  • Make versus buy: buy bare boards, displays, drives, system memory, enclosures and print; integrate systems and manufacture custom logic and AION 4116 display RAM.
  • Engineering qualification: future CAD and masks do not prove video RAM retention, ERA timing, PCB integrity or database recovery.
  • 1981 demand: change the extrapolation before committing expansion capital.

Capital schedule

Production and distribution footprint

Factory floor sizing uses 10 labor-hours per average basket, two shifts, 22 working days and 80% productive use. Bare-board manufacture, mechanical parts and print are reserved at suppliers. Areas below are maintained footprint, not areas to add every quarter.

Production boundary

  • San Jose: incoming inspection, ESD storage, through-hole population/wave solder, board test, system integration, burn-in, manuals and final packing.
  • Suppliers: qualified bare PCBs, molded/metal enclosures, CRT/display assemblies, drive/printer mechanisms, standard components and bulk print.
  • Supplier capacity: reserve 0.6 m² bare-board area, five cartons, 500 printed pages and three media sets per system basket. These are routing allowances; release drawings and edition counts replace them before purchase.
  • Academy: up to 24 dedicated centres, then dealer-hosted teaching rooms; nine machines per classroom.

Growth-stage equipment schedule

Installed allowances include 25% installation. Values in this tool table are constant 1978 USD; quarterly purchases are escalated to purchase year. Tool rates are assumed, not guaranteed specifications. Each critical group includes one redundant tool in addition to 65% OEE.

Capacity calculation

Good packaged dies per wafer = usable gross dies × die yield × package yield. Wafer starts = required packaged chips ÷ good packaged dies per wafer. Peak-month loading adds 5% commercial headroom and a 5% engineering/monitor/training allowance. Tool capacity = tool count × hourly rate × 720 hours × 65% OEE ÷ visits; one tool is removed for the redundancy check.
