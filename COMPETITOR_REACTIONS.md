# Tandy cooperation, Apple response and IBM software delay

These are accepted alternative-story decisions. Exact dates below are selected scenario dates unless explicitly described otherwise; they are not newly discovered historical facts. Canonical events are in `logix_aion_story.json`; source context, date policy and forecast adjustments are in `market_reactions.json`.

## Tandy Model II agreement

The real product's May 1979 announcement, June NCC demonstration/order period and October US shipping window anchor the scenario. No documented engineering design-freeze date has been established. The story deliberately places agreement and interface reservation during a late-1978 design window; the December integration baseline is invented for this scenario.

| Date | Scenario decision or milestone |
|---|---|
| November 6, 1978 | Adam opens confidential talks after Aurelia's launch and growing sales. Demonstrate Abacus and propose official CP/M plus FlexiBUS in exchange for a supported port. |
| November 27, 1978 | Tandy, Logix, DRI and AION sign the cooperation agreement. |
| December 15, 1978 | Agree the compatibility design baseline and reserve the slot, power and interface budget. |
| February 19, 1979 | Review CP/M BIOS, native display/keyboard adapter, Abacus document compatibility and FlexiBUS prototypes. |
| May 30, 1979 | Public Model II announcement includes planned official CP/M, Abacus and a supported FlexiBUS option. Orders are not shipments. |
| June 4, 1979 | NCC demonstration on Model II hardware; distinguish prototypes from released customer configurations. |
| October 15, 1979 | Scenario-selected customer release date within the historical October shipping window, after qualification. |

Tandy preserves its native video, keyboard, disk architecture, conventional ports, branding and customer relationship. A Model II-specific internal FlexiBUS board, provisionally two ports, provides an additional interface; the S-100 card is not mechanically/electrically interchangeable. No Logix video chip, video RAM or monitor is required.

DRI provides CP/M-80 and required platform services; Logix provides reference engineering and initial AION silicon. Tandy officially distributes/supports the configuration and trains its own staff. AION funds, qualifies and maintains Abacus, correcting native UI, disk, print, ROM/banking and memory incompatibilities. Target a 64 KB system and publish measured resource limits. Share document semantics and editing conventions; do not promise identical Aurelia binary compatibility, capacity or speed. A native Tandy printer remains usable; FlexiBUS hardware is not technically mandatory for every Abacus operation.

Licenses are non-exclusive. Tandy may source or manufacture compatible peripherals. Software distribution/minimum-purchase terms and royalties remain unspecified. The customer need not attend Logix Academy or purchase Logix-branded peripherals. AION handles application defects, DRI OS escalation and Tandy first-line service. Initial supply cannot rely on SMC parts before their qualification.

External platform, CP/M, FlexiBUS and Abacus edition records appear in the technical catalog and Abacus page. Their Tandy-owned BOMs, retail prices and OEM economics are excluded from Logix manufacturing/retail totals until separately defined. No pre-NCC Model II customer deliveries or Abacus licenses are counted.

## Apple: software, distribution and selective cooperation

- **July 17, 1978:** coordinate business-software recruitment, demonstration systems and engineering assistance. Disk II development already predates Aurelia; the divergence is commercial/software priority.
- **September 18, 1978:** establish selected dealer business demonstrations, complete quotations and introductory lessons.
- **October 15, 1978:** expand the existing software-partner search, continuing the July initiative.
- **January 16, 1979:** retain the scenario Peachtree accounting agreement, while pursuing spreadsheet/database suppliers separately.
- **February 8, 1979:** retain native Apple expansion cooperation; Apple gains useful third-party products while protecting separate branding/support and its own interfaces.
- **April 16, 1979:** promote complete Apple II/Disk II business configurations and software/training offers. Do not assert an early VisiCalc shipment or pull the March 1980 Apple–Peachtree package into 1979.
- **June 4, 1979:** retain the existing Apple/S-100 expansion demonstrations and licenses.
- **September 17, 1979:** increase the priority of software, peripheral and customer-data compatibility in successor requirements. This does not guarantee a corrected successor or an early Lisa/GUI launch.

The precise Peachtree and other relationship dates are scenario commitments, not claims about historical Apple contracts. No proprietary video adoption or CP/M-6502 commitment is introduced.

## AIONix and IBM's software critical path

**February 1, 1978:** begin preliminary AIONix architecture and portability work with a separately maintained CP/M/Aurelia release effort. **September 12, 1978:** formalise funding, staffing and ownership. Existing AIONix ownership, Gary's equity and 1982 porting rights remain; preliminary engineering does not change the product's public introduction.

**July 5, 1979:** IBM confidentially benchmarks business workflows, dealer/service channels and OS suppliers. **December 10, 1979:** authorise the project because Logix, Tandy, Apple and their partners are establishing business-computing expectations outside IBM; remove the implausible Logix–Apple duopoly rationale.

**February 15, 1980:** sourcing review finds no committed qualified x86 CP/M delivery on IBM's timetable. DRI's funded AIONix and existing CP/M-80 work receive priority. Its independence does not imply it has spare resources for x86. The Tandy Z80 adaptation reuses existing investment and does not contradict this decision.

Paterson's recruitment removes the historical SCP/86-DOS acquisition opportunity; Letwin's absence reduces Microsoft's available expertise. These constraints do not make another OS technically impossible. DRI is not contractually barred from every IBM transaction.

**July 15, 1980:** after supplier selection, IBM funds an alternative OS programme and stabilises application interfaces. Supplier identity and OS name are deliberately unresolved. **August 17, 1981:** OS/application qualification and support preparation remain on the critical path despite substantial hardware readiness. Early 1982 stays the central commercial target; late 1981 is a possible recovery, not categorically impossible. No completed 1982 launch is asserted outside the current timeline.

The original 3–4 month prototype and 11–13 month manufacturing-ready targets apply to hardware. They do not establish a complete sellable software platform.

## Forecast consequences before NCC 1979

Keep the reduced-profit prices unchanged. Explicit incremental event adjustments replace the earlier generic competitor-response allowance:

- Central Apple displacement: 1,800 Aurelia purchases over the launch year, including 250 during 1978. These are competitor purchases, not vanished market demand.
- Late-May public Tandy announcement: 150 Aurelia purchases deferred beyond the cutoff. Confidential November talks cause no immediate public demand effect.
- May portable-extension/compatibility confidence: 150 additional Aurelia purchases.
- June NCC demonstration, October Tandy release, later Apple review and confidential IBM developments: zero effect on earlier completed sales.

Before the BPI campaign revision, the central Aurelia forecast was **10,350 in calendar 1978** and **28,200 during June 1978–May 1979**, approximately **10.37%** of the assumed expanded US launch-year market. Displayed precision supports arithmetic, not forecasting accuracy: interpret this as roughly 28,000, with scenarios spanning roughly 23,000–33,000. The new/accelerated share of Apple-transferred purchases remains in competitor totals, so displacement does not falsely shrink the market.

Tandy's later Abacus revenue and any longer-term ecosystem benefit require a separate post-NCC forecast. IBM's later delay is not credited as extra sales in 1978–early 1979. Existing Academy capacity recommendations remain prudent capacity targets rather than automatic openings.

## BPI partner campaign revision

The independent BPI programme adds 1,200 central-case Aurelia deliveries, producing 10,750 in 1978 and 29,400 through May 1979 (10.78% assumed US unit share). Apple displacement and Tandy deferral/confidence assumptions remain intact; BPI is not a second blanket market-expansion bonus. Strong-case demand is capped at 34,000 deliveries. BPI licence receipts are separately accounted. No BPI Model II release is implied by the Abacus agreement. See BPI_PARTNER_STRATEGY.md.


## NCC 1979 standardisation programme

NCC_1979_CAMPAIGN.md records accepted recruitment and marketing plans. North Star and Vector Graphic are priority prospects; Cromemco, Altos, Intertec and Heath have conditional/selective paths. No actual commitment or supported new OEM release is invented. Targets are one additional OEM shipping by end-1979, another qualifying, and three to five non-Logix OEMs by NCC 1980 including Tandy. Independent video adoption remains optional. These targets do not add sales retroactively before June or grant a Tandy BPI port. Software portfolios, host resource limits and native interfaces remain independent.
