# Narrative

This an alternative reality story to be analsysed:

(Let't call the real history as base scenario and the story below the alternative scenario.)

# Time Traveler story

There is an 19yrs old individual who gained on 2025-12-07 a special power letting him to travel in time.  
Vincent time travels to 1972-01-01, he obtains the identity of 19yrs old Adam Pleskot living in UK that looks very similar to him. Adam after graduating from high school, has just died along with his parents in a car accident, so time traveler was able to replace Adam before anybody noticed. Let’s start calling time traveler Adam from this moment on.

On 1972-02-06 Adam leaves UK and goes to Germany where he wins LOTTO 6aus49 1 million DM (using his superpower), he exchanges it into ~310000\$ and prepares to relocate. On 1972-03-01 he moves to the US and begins NYSE investing and speculation, rapidly multiplying his wealth. His four-year Harvard economics studies run from autumn 1972 through June 1976, with annual study periods detailed in the index above (the first two fall start dates remain provisional). In parallel to that he speculates on NYSE and multiplies his money, he uses his special time-travel abilities foresee the future and keeps it in secret.

He starts on 1973-01-01 a small office with 3 workers and PDP-11/40 setup with 3 terminals. Workers manually capture NY Times information about NYSE stock market prices, sales and other statistics, they also capture in the form of measures sentiments for NYSE companies and their markets based on articles in the newspapers. These captured data can be analyzed and used for predictions. Adam will use prediction algorithms as a fake justification for unusual successes in investing.

Between 1973 and 1975 he spreads his investment activities to London, Frankfurt, Honk Kong and Tokyo stock exchanges.

He starts on 1975-01-01 three companies to change the original history of personal computing:

1)  Logix Tech in San Jose US specialized in Software Development and Computer manufacturing

2)  AION Labs in Cambridge UK - a to create a small, technologically conservative semiconductor operation that may grow into a chip fab

3)  AION Software in Copenhagen - focused on business applications and compliers development

Adam aims at competing with IBM on personal computing standard. His companies are focused at developing products to be presented at NCC conferences starting from 1978 and COMDEX conferences starting from 1979 and then got manufactured at maximum possible scale to gain market coverage enough to dictate standards.

Adam doesn’t play fair he took from 2020s equipment that would allow him to design and beam lithography masks for chip manufacturing and on 1975-02-01 established a secret facility away from his three companies:

- CAD WORKSTATION with Virtuoso, Spectre, Xcelium , Genus, Innovus, Tempus, Quantus, Pegasus

- HEIDELBERG DWL 66+ Laser Lithography System

# 1978 NCC in June - Logix Aurelia to be presented 

The first goal is to create an 8-bit personal computer called Logix Aurelia and present on 1978 NCC. To get market recognition and built trust and the network of loyal users, software developers and peripheral manufacturers.

But first Adam must kick of his companies, he hires people, create software development environments, computer manufacturing and R&D, and small, technologically conservative semiconductor operation.

Given that Adam knows history architects and talented technologists:

- He recruits Gordon Letwin on 1977-06-01 from the place it was working at this time (in this history Gordon never joins Microsoft).

- He recruits Tim Patterson on 1977-07-15 (in this history, Tim never works on QDOS) nor is available for Microsoft

Adam also sign agreements:

- on 1976-09-01 with Digital Research to work with them on extended version of CP/M.  
  Adam offered CP/M to retain the right to Implement TUI libraries and FlexiBUS support to any CP/M version for non-Logix computer.

- on 1977-09-01 with Micro Peripherals, Inc. to agree manufacturing purchasing OEM MP-40 units bare mechanisms in order to equip them with FlexiBUS controller

- on 1978-02-05 with Computerland for selling computers with a 20-30% margin, Computer is planned as a primary sales channel

On 1977-02-15 Adam makes an offer to the owners BPI Systems: \$150K in cash, + 33% of shares in the new AION BPI Systems: the new company will focus on selling products to Logix computers. BPI former owners will remain running the company but along with Adam’s people. Adam’s people will help to port BPI products to C Language, include database engine, change UI to Logix TUI.

On 1978-03-25 Logix Computer Academy company is brought to life: training facilities rent in different US cities. Number and size of facilities adjusted to market demand.

On 1978-06-05 at NCC 1978 Logix launches Aurelia as a complete personal-computing platform, supported by AION software, peripherals, support services, developer programs and the Logix Computer Academy.

Logix, AION and BPI jointly premiere the BPI business-application suite as part of the initial Aurelia offering at NCC 1978 on 1978-06-05. The suite is sold separately rather than automatically included in each base computer bundle.

BPI product training and direct sales through Logix Computer Academy become available on 1978-06-12, after the NCC presentation.

## The market and competition response between NCC 1978 and NCC 1979

- DRI on 1978-11-10: Commits to developing a portable CP/M extension incorporating Logix's TUI libraries, with the intention of offering it to other computer manufacturers.

- Apple on 1978-10-15: Start searching for a partnership to provide Apple II business software and disk-based capabilities, including spreadsheet and database applications.

- Other companies actively monitor situation but haven’t made any significant decisions or taken any important actions in response to Logix/AION activity and offering.

- Personal computer US market is 10-15% bigger through Logix offering, Logix Computer Academy personal computer popularization, seamless integration with peripherals and more accessible user interfaces.

- DRI Releases on 1979-05-05 another of CP/M version that offers the Logix TUI and FlexiBUS libraries to other computer manufacturers, preserving the opportunity to expand CP/M's software ecosystem.

- Apple seeks for Apple II's business software catalogue, especially spreadsheet, database and accounting products. Unfortunately for them VisiCalc is less advanced, in terms of spreadsheet size and available features. On 1979-01-16 Apple starts partnership with Peachtree Software to ensure their computer has tailored offering for business users.

# NCC June 1979 - Apple II extensions and Microsoft BASIC

On 1979-02-08, Logix contacted Apple Computer seeking technical cooperation in developing peripherals that would allow users to exchange files between the Apple II and Logix Aurelia. Logix requested:

- permission to use the relevant Apple II technical documentation;

- access to Apple II machines and assistance with compatibility testing; and

- engineering consultation concerning Apple II peripheral and Disk II compatibility.

Apple agreed to provide limited technical cooperation and compatibility assistance, while Logix remained responsible for the design and development of its hardware and software.

At NCC on 1979-06-04, Logix presents the Logix Apple II Expansion Card and Logix FlexiDisk II, with an Apple representative participating in the demonstration.

On 1979-06-04 at NCC 1979, Microsoft releases Microsoft BASIC for Aurelia as licensed third-party software, developed in cooperation with AION for enhanced CP/M and Logix TUI.

## The market and competition response until 1979-12-02

- Apple continues to permit technical cooperation, supports compatibility testing and lets independent developers sell compatible products. It does not manufacture FlexiBUS devices itself or officially designate FlexiBUS as an Apple standard.

- IBM starts confidential investigation on 1979-07-05 of presonal computer market and Conduct a formal competitive and financial assessment.

# COMDEX on 1979-12-03 

AION presents Pulse and Abacus 2.0, while Logix launches Aurelia+ and makes corresponding training available through the Logix Computer Academy.

## The market and competition response until May 1980

- IBM’s investigation indicates that partitioned personal computer market started consolidating around Logix and Apple. On 1979-12-10 IBM starts a project to establish credibility in business computing, relationships with corporate customers, a global service organization and substantial engineering and manufacturing resources.

  - Their plan: Functional prototype in 3–4 months; manufacturing-ready design in 11–13 months; commercial launch targeted for early 1982.

  - Objective Approximately \$1,500 for an entry configuration, with practical business configurations priced higher.

  - IBM intends to create themselves a 16bit operating x86, possibly using external contractors.  
    (taking into account that Microsoft is incapable of building operating system, and DRI offering is done in cooperation with Logix for 8-bit machines only)

- Apple and Peachtree starts on 1980-03-15 the Small-business package offering that covers: accounting, potentially including general ledger, accounts payable/receivable, inventory, invoicing, and payroll.  
  It contains a simplified setup, Apple II-oriented interface, documentation, and support for business users.

# NCC May 1980 – Logix Artemis release

Logix Tech, AION Labs and AION Software work jointly on designing, constructing and manufacturing Logix Artemis. Massive marketing campaign precedes presentation telling about new era of personal computing.

- Logix Tech buys a few PDP-11 machines develops a processor emulator based on the blueprint that heavily reminds ARM v1 architecture but implements called ERA1  
  (Extreme RISC Architecture).

- Logix Tech also works on designing the computer architecture - Logix Artemis

- On 1978-09-12 I (on behalf of AION Software and Logix) sign the agreement with DRI to work jointly with on the operating **AIONix** system for ERA processor (assembler and c):

  - DRI will dedicate resources together with AION Software, provide their expertise and operating system development experience

  - AION Software will cover the DRI costs and expenses. AION Software will become the owner controlling the AIONix roadmap.

  - Gary Kildal will receive in tranches 8% of Logix equities

  - DRI will obtain in 1982 the right to port AIONix to non-ERA processors. DRI can freely re-use key AIONix components, but not the Logix computer architecture or bus/IO standards.

  - AION Software department working on **AIONix** must move from Copenhagen to Logix offices to be based close to DRI headquarters.

- AION Labs uses provided processor blueprints and designs masks. Designs from scratch remaining custom chips.

- Logix designs and constructs motherboard. develops BIOS, designs and constructs other elements of the computer.

- On 1979-07-01 Logix and DRI undertake development of a CP/M-80 compatibility module so that existing Aurelia software can continue to run on Artemis.

- On 1979-07-01 AION Software undertakes preparation of Artemis software and development tools: it develops AIONix with DRI, ports the established business applications to Artemis, and creates the native ERA C development environment and a larger Business Tools suite.

 

On 1980-05-19 at NCC, Artemis is launched commercially together with AIONix, compatible peripherals and expansions, developer tooling, support services and new Artemis training through the Logix Computer Academy.

# COMDEX November 1980 – AION 32-bit software focus

On 1980-11-19 AION presents Artemis-exclusive Abacus 3.0 and Pulse 3.0. From this point, major AION software development treats the 32-bit Artemis platform as the primary target, while the Aurelia line receives maintenance and simplified refreshes.

# NCC May 1981 – Logix networked business computing

On 1981-05-04 Logix presents its TCP/IP networking platform, the Artemis PRO server, networking upgrades for both Aurelia CP/M and Artemis/AIONix, and a new client-server generation of BPI built around a robust relational database. Networking becomes part of the standard Artemis direction rather than an isolated peripheral feature.
