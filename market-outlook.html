Manufacturing and port-development boundaries

  • CP/M-86 does not exist; DRI prioritizes CP/M-80/TUI and AIONix/PRO.
  • Adam owns 30% of independent DRI; IBM sees a strategic conflict with competitor Logix.
  • Paterson does not create QDOS; IBM commissions its own alternative disk OS, with qualification risk.
  • DRI non-ERA port rights activate in 1982; 68k requires suitable MMU/system design; 286 enables feasibility, not an immediate release.
  • ERAv1 remains 3 um. June 1982 repaired 3 um RAM costs remain the volume cost basis through year-end; 2 um pilot savings await repeatable-production approval.
  • Adam foresees demand peaks, not engineering success: reserve 5% commercial headroom, retain OEE, one-down redundancy and qualification gates.

1982 includes $700,000 of process capital, the $600,000 memory-engineering provision net of allocated existing payroll, $144,000 pilot operations, and DRI 68k prototype work. No 68k licence receipts or 1983 sales are booked. Larger server storage remains unpriced; Team adoption is limited to small repositories.
